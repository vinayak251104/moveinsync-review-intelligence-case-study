# MoveInSync Employee Commute App --- Review Intelligence

An exploratory analytics and review-triage case study built around
Google Play written reviews for the MoveInSync Employee commute app. The
project combines descriptive analysis, interactive visualizations,
heuristic text exploration, app-version comparisons, developer-response
analysis, and an optional AI-classifier pilot.

> **Important:** This project analyzes user-written reviews. Reviews are
> self-selected feedback and are not a representative survey of every
> employee, app session, or commute. Findings are descriptive unless
> independently validated.

## Project objectives

The goal is to turn a noisy collection of public app reviews into a
repeatable workflow for understanding user experience and identifying
areas that deserve investigation.

The project is designed to:

1.  Profile the dataset and assess basic data quality.
2.  Understand the distribution of written-review ratings.
3.  Track monthly review volume and rating changes.
4.  Examine developer reply coverage and reply timing.
5.  Explore review text to surface recurring product and operational
    signals.
6.  Compare ratings across app versions where enough reviews are
    available.
7.  Display AI-generated review labels as a separate, exploratory
    classification pilot.
8.  Convert descriptive signals into investigation questions and
    potential operational actions.

This is an analytics and prioritization aid, not a production incident
detector or a definitive measure of customer satisfaction.

## Data sources and fields

### 1. Raw written reviews --- `reviews_employee_en_in.csv`

This is the primary source for EDA. The dataset used during development
contained 8,355 written reviews spanning approximately 14 November 2023
to 28 October 2026. Re-run the notebook or dashboard against the exact
local file to verify the counts and date range in your copy.

The workflow uses these fields when available:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Field                               Use
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  `reviewId`                          Review identity, duplicate checks,
                                      and optional joins

  `content`                           User-written review text

  `score`                             Star rating, expected to be 1--5

  `at`                                Review timestamp and temporal
                                      aggregation

  `thumbsUpCount`                     Public engagement signal

  `replyContent`                      Whether the developer replied and
                                      reply-text inspection

  `repliedAt`                         Reply-delay calculation

  `reviewCreatedVersion`              App-version comparison

  `appVersion`                        Optional version metadata, if
                                      present

  `scraped_at`                        Data extraction metadata, if
                                      present
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

The application creates derived fields such as normalized review text,
word and character counts, reply-presence flags, month, weekday, and
reply delay.

### 2. Rating metadata --- `rating_snapshots.csv` (optional)

This file stores app-level rating metadata captured at a point in time.
It can provide context for the Play Store's displayed rating and rating
count.

**Do not equate the app-level Play Store rating with the average score
of the scraped written reviews.** They can use different populations,
denominators, and aggregation rules.

### 3. AI-classified pilot --- `reviews_classified.csv` (optional)

This file contains the output of the separate classifier pipeline.
Expected fields may include:

-   `reviewId`
-   `primary`
-   `secondary`
-   `owner`
-   `severity`
-   `issue`
-   `score` (if included in the classifier output)

The dashboard displays the available fields but does not treat the pilot
as representative of the full raw dataset. If you also retain a
recovered or alternate classifier output, preserve it as a separate
version and document differences rather than silently merging
conflicting labels.

## Workflow

### Step 1 --- Load and inspect

-   Read the raw CSV.
-   Check required columns and data types.
-   Parse review and reply timestamps.
-   Convert rating and engagement fields to numeric values.
-   Inspect missing values, blank review text, duplicate IDs, invalid
    ratings, and timestamp anomalies.

### Step 2 --- Create analysis fields

The EDA and dashboard derive fields from the raw columns:

-   `content_clean`: whitespace-normalized review text.
-   `reply_clean`: whitespace-normalized developer response.
-   `has_text`: whether a review contains non-empty text.
-   `has_reply`: whether a developer response is present.
-   `review_word_count`: number of word-like tokens in review text.
-   `review_char_count`: character count.
-   `reply_delay_hours`: elapsed time between review and reply.
-   `month`: calendar month of the review.
-   `weekday`: day of week of the review.

The raw text is retained. Normalization is for analysis, not an attempt
to rewrite the user's meaning.

### Step 3 --- Describe rating patterns

-   Count reviews by star rating.
-   Calculate mean, median, and standard deviation.
-   Calculate the share of 1-star, 1--2-star, and 4--5-star reviews.
-   Compare monthly averages with monthly review volume.
-   Track one-star and low-rating shares by month.

### Step 4 --- Analyze developer responses

-   Calculate the share of reviews with a response.
-   Compare reply coverage across star ratings.
-   Calculate reply delays only when timestamps are usable.
-   Compare ratings for replied and unreplied reviews as a descriptive
    slice.

Reply presence is not evidence that the issue was resolved. The reply
comparison is also subject to selection bias: developers may choose
which reviews to answer.

### Step 5 --- Explore text and engagement

-   Tokenize review text using a simple regular expression.
-   Remove a small, transparent list of common words.
-   Count frequent terms.
-   Calculate review length and thumbs-up patterns.
-   Inspect highly engaged reviews and examples from keyword matches.

This is lightweight text exploration, not a full
natural-language-processing pipeline. It does not use embeddings, topic
models, sentiment models, or a trained text classifier in the EDA stage.

### Step 6 --- Create keyword issue signals

The notebook and dashboard use phrase lists to find reviews mentioning
broad areas such as:

-   App access, login, OTP, loading, and crashes
-   Booking and scheduling
-   Tracking, GPS, maps, and location
-   Cab availability and no-shows
-   Delays and waiting
-   Driver behaviour and safety
-   Support and responses
-   UX and usability

A review can match multiple groups. A keyword match does not prove that
the review is a complaint or that the issue occurred. The examples shown
in the dashboard should be manually inspected before turning these
counts into formal findings.

### Step 7 --- Compare app versions

For versions with at least a configurable minimum number of reviews, the
dashboard reports review volume, average rating, one-star share, and
1--2-star share. The minimum-volume threshold reduces the visibility of
very small groups, but does not remove exposure or selection bias.

### Step 8 --- Review the classifier pilot

The classifier tab summarizes the labels that exist in the optional CSV,
including primary categories, suggested ownership, severity, and
category-by-rating counts where those fields are present.

The pilot is kept separate from full-dataset EDA. Label frequencies from
a small, date-ordered sample must not be projected onto the entire
dataset.

### Step 9 --- Export and interpret

The notebook exports CSV tables to `outputs/`. The dashboard also offers
downloads for selected tables and filtered review rows. Use these
outputs to support a report or presentation, and record the filters used
when interpreting a chart.

## Exploratory data analysis methodology

### Rating distribution

Ratings are treated as ordinal scores from 1 through 5. The mean is
useful for tracking changes but does not fully describe the
distribution, so the analysis also reports the median and the share of
low and high ratings.

Definitions:

-   **One-star share** = count of 1-star reviews ÷ reviews with valid
    ratings.
-   **Low-rating share** = count of 1- or 2-star reviews ÷ reviews with
    valid ratings.
-   **High-rating share** = count of 4- or 5-star reviews ÷ reviews with
    valid ratings.

The denominator should be stated in reports. When using dashboard
filters, these percentages apply to the filtered population unless
explicitly described as full-dataset metrics.

### Monthly trends

Reviews are grouped by calendar month using the review timestamp. For
each month, the workflow calculates:

-   Review count
-   Mean and median rating
-   One-star count and percentage
-   1--2-star count and percentage
-   4--5-star count and percentage

Review volume is plotted alongside rating trends because small monthly
samples can be volatile. A low-scoring month is a signal for
investigation, not proof of a product regression or operational
incident.

### Developer-response analysis

-   **Reply coverage** = reviews with non-empty developer reply text ÷
    reviews in scope.
-   **Reply delay** = reply timestamp minus review timestamp.
-   Negative delays are treated as timestamp anomalies and should not be
    interpreted as real response times.
-   Delay distributions are restricted in some visualizations to a
    bounded window so a small number of extreme values does not dominate
    the chart.

Response coverage does not measure the quality, helpfulness, or
resolution of a reply.

### Review text and engagement

Word count and character count are basic proxies for review length.
`thumbsUpCount` is used as a public engagement indicator. Neither length
nor thumbs-up count is a direct measure of issue severity or
representativeness.

The top-term analysis is based on regular-expression tokenization and a
small stopword list. It does not understand context, negation, sarcasm,
spelling variation, or multi-word meaning beyond the explicit phrase
matching.

### Keyword matching

Each issue group is a list of phrases. A review is counted once per
group if it contains any phrase in that group. Therefore:

-   Group counts overlap.
-   Percentages across groups do not sum to 100%.
-   A phrase can appear in a positive review.
-   Synonyms and misspellings may be missed.
-   The counts should be called **keyword matches** or **exploratory
    signals**, not confirmed issue counts.

Manual review of examples is required before presenting a keyword group
as a product finding.

### App-version comparison

Version summaries are grouped by the `reviewCreatedVersion` field when
available. The analysis reports review volume and rating metrics for
versions meeting a minimum-volume threshold.

These are observational comparisons. Versions may differ in exposure
duration, user population, rollout status, and missing metadata. A lower
average rating does not establish that a release caused a problem.

### Classifier analysis

The classifier is an assistive taxonomy layer intended to organize
free-text feedback into categories, ownership hypotheses, severity, and
short issue descriptions. The dashboard reports the labels that are
present; it does not independently verify them.

Accuracy should only be reported after comparing predictions against a
manually annotated reference set. Useful evaluation measures include
per-class precision, recall, F1 score, confusion matrix, and agreement
on ownership/severity. A small set of spot checks is not sufficient to
claim high accuracy.

## How to run the project

### Requirements

-   Python 3.10 or later is recommended.
-   The raw review CSV must be available locally.
-   Internet access is not required to run the dashboard after
    dependencies are installed.

### 1. Open a terminal in the project folder

In PowerShell, use quotes around paths containing spaces:

``` powershell
cd "C:\Users\YourName\Downloads\MoveInSync"
```

### 2. Install dependencies

``` powershell
py -m pip install --upgrade pip
py -m pip install streamlit pandas plotly numpy matplotlib jupyter
```

If the `py` launcher is unavailable, try replacing `py` with `python`.

### 3. Start the dashboard

``` powershell
py -m streamlit run app.py
```

Using `py -m streamlit` can avoid issues where the `streamlit`
executable is not on PATH.

### 4. Run the EDA notebook

Launch Jupyter:

``` powershell
py -m jupyter notebook
```

Open `MoveInSync_Exploratory_Analysis.ipynb` and run the cells from top
to bottom. The notebook looks for the CSVs in the current folder or a
`data/` subfolder and writes tables to `outputs/`.

### Common setup issues

**`streamlit` is not recognized**

Install the dependencies and run Streamlit as a Python module:

``` powershell
py -m pip install streamlit pandas plotly numpy
py -m streamlit run app.py
```

**File not found**

Confirm `app.py` and the raw CSV are in the folder from which you
launched Streamlit, or put the CSV in a `data/` folder. Check spelling
and file extensions.

**Path contains spaces**

Wrap the full path in double quotes:

``` powershell
cd "C:\Users\YourName\Downloads\MoveInSync\data"
```

**Different Python environments**

If installation succeeds but imports fail, use the same Python launcher
for installation and execution (`py -m pip ...` followed by
`py -m streamlit ...`).

## Interpretation and limitations

### 1. Self-selection bias

People who write app-store reviews may differ from people who do not.
Review data cannot directly estimate satisfaction across all employees
or all commutes.

### 2. Written-review mean versus app rating

The average of the scraped written reviews is not the Play Store's
aggregate rating. They have different denominators and potentially
different aggregation rules.

### 3. No causal inference

Monthly rating dips, app-version differences, reply status, and keyword
counts are associations or descriptive patterns. They do not prove a
release, vendor, driver, or operational process caused an outcome.

### 4. Keyword rules are simplistic

Phrase matching cannot reliably understand context, negation, sarcasm,
spelling errors, or synonyms. Categories overlap and counts must not be
summed as if they were mutually exclusive.

### 5. Timestamp quality

Missing, malformed, or inconsistent timestamps can affect trend and
reply-delay metrics. Negative reply delays are anomalies that require
investigation.

### 6. Version metadata may be incomplete

Some reviews may have no version information. Different versions may
have different exposure durations and user populations, making
comparisons imperfect.

### 7. Classifier pilot is limited

A 100-review date-ordered pilot is not a random or representative
sample. Its category proportions must not be extrapolated to all
reviews. AI-generated ownership and severity labels may be wrong and
should be reviewed.

### 8. No validated resolution metric

The available review data shows whether a developer reply exists, not
whether the user's problem was fixed. Measuring resolution would require
linked support or operational records.

### 9. Privacy and responsible reporting

Review text can contain personal information. Avoid exposing unnecessary
identifiers or sensitive text in public reports. Use only data you are
permitted to process, and do not commit secrets or API keys.

### 10. Dashboard interpretation

The global filters change the population used by most charts. Record the
filters when quoting results. The dashboard is exploratory and should be
paired with manual validation and domain knowledge before business
decisions are made.

## Recommended next steps

1.  **Validate issue themes:** manually review samples from the keyword
    groups and classifier categories.
2.  **Create a labelled reference set:** annotate a stratified sample
    across star ratings and issue types, then evaluate classifier
    precision and recall.
3.  **Prioritize by impact:** combine frequency with severity, employee
    impact, operational risk, and confidence---not raw keyword count
    alone.
4.  **Investigate time anomalies:** align low-scoring months with
    release notes, commute incidents, holidays, shift changes, and
    service logs.
5.  **Measure resolution:** if access is available, connect review
    themes to support tickets, incident data, or service-level metrics.
6.  **Improve monitoring:** track low-rating share, recurring validated
    themes, reply coverage, and time-to-resolution over time.
7.  **Keep evidence separate from hypotheses:** label findings as
    observed, inferred, or requiring validation.

## Summary

This project provides a reproducible path from raw employee commute app
reviews to descriptive metrics, issue-discovery signals, and an
interactive dashboard. Its value lies in helping a product or operations
team decide **what to investigate next**, not in claiming that text
heuristics or a small AI pilot have already established definitive root
causes.

Use the full dataset for descriptive rating and response analysis,
validate text-derived signals manually, and treat the classifier as a
separate pilot until it has been evaluated against human-labelled data.
